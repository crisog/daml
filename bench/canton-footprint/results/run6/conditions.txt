7. Why the Postgres recovery is slower on our machine than on boat.dev (../../fsync-cost.py)
   One recovery on our machine, from the Postgres log (run2, all-capped-4cores-kill-r1):
     36.7 s  "syncing data directory (fsync)": Postgres calls fsync on each of
             its 12,208 files (15 databases) before it reads its write-ahead log
      0.5 s  redo
     13.5 s  the checkpoint at the end of recovery (16,247 buffers)
     50.8 s  from "database system was interrupted" to "ready to accept connections"
   On boat.dev the first step takes 0.3 s for the same files (transfer-ab logs:
   0.3 s from "was interrupted" to "automatic recovery in progress").
   fsync-cost.py writes 2,000 files of 8 KiB, then calls fsync on each one, two
   times. Both machines: ext4, the same filesystem as the Docker volumes.
     machine                                   new files   the same files again
     ours (Crucial P3 Plus NVMe, bare metal)   2.99 ms     1.97 ms
     boat.dev (virtio disk, 4 vCPU machine)    4.47 ms     0.024 ms
   The boat.dev value for new files was taken while LocalNet restarted on that
   machine. An fsync that has nothing to write costs 2 ms on our disk and
   0.02 ms on the boat.dev disk. Most of the 12,208 files have nothing to write
   at recovery: 12,208 x 2 to 3 ms is 24 to 37 s, and 12,208 x 0.024 ms is 0.3 s.
   Inferred, not measured: ext4 sends a cache flush to the disk for each fsync,
   our NVMe takes about 2 ms for each flush, and the virtual disk returns at
   once when it has nothing to flush.

8. A Postgres setting for the slow recovery (syncfs.tsv, syncfs-alter.tsv)
   For the report to Splice, not for the post. The case is the slow-recovery
   restart of run5: 8,000 extra tables (about 44,200 files), kill, shipped
   limits, cores 0-3. With the shipped settings it failed 2 of 2 in run5.
   a. ../../localnet-syncfs.yaml adds "-c recovery_init_sync_method=syncfs" to
      the command of the postgres service. Result: failed 2 of 2, and the log
      still says "syncing data directory (fsync)". Cause, from
      docker/postgres/postgres-entrypoint.sh of the release: the entrypoint
      calls docker_temp_server_start at each start, before it runs the command.
      That temporary server runs the recovery, with pg_ctl -w (60 s), and it
      does not get the options of the command.
   b. The same case with the setting stored in the data directory before the
      kill (ALTER SYSTEM SET recovery_init_sync_method = syncfs, which writes
      postgresql.auto.conf). Result: the fsync step is gone (0.06 s), and the
      restart still failed 2 of 2. The 8,000 extra tables are new files that
      the checkpoint at the end of recovery must sync, so this case is not a
      fair test of the setting.
   c. syncfs-normal.tsv: normal kill restarts (no extra tables), shipped
      limits, three with the shipped Postgres settings and three with the
      setting of b, in turns. Seconds from "database system was interrupted"
      to "ready to accept connections":
        shipped   38.1   51.0   did not start at the first attempt (pg_ctl timeout)
        syncfs    14.1   14.1   14.1
      With the setting, the sync step takes less than 0.1 s, and the 13.5 s
      that are left are the checkpoint at the end of recovery. All six
      networks kept their ledger.
